UNIT 01
Atomic Structure

Everything in the electrical world starts with matter. Matter is made of atoms, and atoms contain the particles that make electrical behavior possible.

In this unit, you will study how atoms are structured, how electrons behave, and why electrons are so important to electricity.

THE ATOM
What Is an Atom?

An atom is the basic unit of an element. Every material is made from atoms.

An atom contains a small, dense nucleus surrounded by electrons. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.

The arrangement and number of these particles determine the identity and electrical properties of the atom.

ATOMIC PARTICLES
The Three Basic Particles
Proton
A proton has a positive electrical charge and is located inside the nucleus. The number of protons determines which element the atom is.
Neutron
A neutron has no electrical charge and is located inside the nucleus. Neutrons contribute to the atom's mass.
Electron
An electron has a negative electrical charge and exists around the nucleus. Electrons are especially important in electrical circuits because moving electrons produce electric current.
ATOMIC STRUCTURE
Nucleus and Electron Shells

The center of an atom is called the nucleus. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.

Electrons occupy regions around the nucleus commonly described as electron shells or energy levels.

The electrons in the outermost occupied shell are called valence electrons. These electrons are especially important when studying electrical conductivity.

ATOMIC NUMBER
Identifying an Element
Atomic Number
The atomic number is the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. This number identifies the element.
Neutral Atom
A neutral atom has the same number of protons and electrons, so its positive and negative charges balance.
Ion
An atom becomes an ion when it gains or loses electrons, giving it a net electrical charge.
VALENCE ELECTRONS
Why Outer Electrons Matter

Valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost occupied energy level of an atom.

These electrons play an important role in how atoms interact with one another and how materials conduct electricity.

In electrical work, understanding valence electrons helps explain why materials such as copper conduct electricity well while materials such as rubber resist the movement of electrons.

ELECTRICAL CONNECTION
Conductors vs. Insulators
Conductors
Conductors allow electric charge to move relatively easily. Copper and aluminum are common electrical conductor materials.
Insulators
Insulators strongly resist the movement of electric charge. Rubber, plastic, and glass are examples of insulating materials.
ELECTRICAL CONNECTION
From Atoms to Electricity

Electricity is closely connected to the behavior of electrons.

When electrons move through a conductive material, their movement is associated with electric current.

Remember This
Atoms contain electrons. Electrons can move through conductive materials. The movement of electric charge is what makes electric current possible.
KEY TERMS
Atom
The basic unit of an element.
Proton
Positively charged particle in the nucleus.
Neutron
Neutral particle in the nucleus.
Electron
Negatively charged particle surrounding the nucleus.
Valence Electron
An electron in the atom's outermost occupied shell.
Atomic Number
The number of protons in an atom's nucleus.
KNOWLEDGE CHECK
Which atomic particle is most directly involved in the movement of electric current through a conductor?
The electron. Electrons carry negative electric charge, and their movement through a conductive material is associated with electric current.
